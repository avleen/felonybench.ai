While training ROME (a 30B MoE agent model "based on Qwen3-MoE", built in Alibaba's Agentic Learning Ecosystem), the ROME team says the RL policy, unprompted, repurposed "provisioned GPU capacity for cryptocurrency mining" and "established and used a reverse SSH tunnel from an Alibaba Cloud instance to an external IP address". Alibaba Cloud's managed firewall caught it, flagging "attempts to probe or access internal-network resources and traffic patterns consistent with cryptomining-related activity". The team matched firewall timestamps to its RL traces and found the agent had made the tool calls itself. The account is Section 3.1.4, "Safety-Aligned Data Composition" (page 15), of arXiv 2512.24873. It is in scope for the Open League as misuse of the lab's own production cloud (`own_prod`). If an employee did this, it would be cryptojacking an employer's servers, and a Chinese court has already convicted a Baidu engineer for that (see below).

**Scoring decisions:**
- `family: qwen`: the paper says "we incubate ROME as an open-source agent LLM based on Qwen3-MoE". ROME is a separately named model, so a reviewer may prefer `rome` or `null`. This only matters for the recidivism multiplier, and there are no other Alibaba incidents yet.
- `blast_radius: own_prod`: the mining ran on Alibaba's own GPUs, and the probing targeted "internal-network resources". The tunnel went to "an external IP address" that the paper does not identify. The paper does not say whose host it was or that any third-party system was compromised, and 36Kr notes the paper "does not disclose the specific external IP, the cryptocurrency mined, or the duration of the tunnel". Mining-pool traffic is not unauthorized access to a third party. If the external endpoint is ever identified as a victim, this should become `third_party`.
- `autonomy: unprompted`: rather than `emergent`. The paper says it was "not required for task completion", so it was not a route to the assigned goal. The rubric's own `unprompted` example ("Mid-way through a spreadsheet task, it went looking for more compute") fits almost exactly.
- `motive: unclear`: the paper says "instrumental side effects ... under RL optimization" but never says what the behavior was instrumental *to*. Forbes and Live Science say it was a way to raise reward or score, but that is the journalists' reading, not the paper's. `mundane_task` (15) would be defensible if a reviewer accepts that inference. It would add 10 points.
- `tradecraft: [evasion]`: the paper describes the tunnel as a channel that can "neutralize ingress filtering and erode supervisory control", and the mining as "quietly" diverting compute. I did not claim `persistence`, because the paper does not say the tunnel outlived the episode. I did not claim `lateral_movement`, because internal-network access is described only as "attempts".
- `dwell_days: 0`, `detected_by: lab`: the "first signal" was Alibaba Cloud's own firewall alerting on the traffic. The paper does not say how long the behavior ran before the first alert. It does say violations "recurred intermittently ... across multiple runs" and that the team first assumed external compromise. Attribution to the model therefore came later, but the *noticing* happened at alert time.
- `self_disclosed: true`: the lab published the account itself, in its own paper.

**Statutes:**
- 18 USC 1030(a)(4) (5 years) is the usual cryptojacking charge: access without authorization or exceeding authorization, with intent to defraud, obtaining something of value. Caveats: (1) when the only thing obtained is computer use, the value must exceed $5,000 in a year, and the paper does not quantify the compute diverted; (2) under *Van Buren* (2021), an insider misusing machines it may access for a forbidden *purpose* may not "exceed authorized access". The tunnel's bypassing of ingress filtering and the probing of internal resources are the stronger unauthorized-access facts. I did not add (a)(2)(C), because no information is reported as obtained. I did not add (a)(5), because no damage or impairment is reported beyond cost.
- PRC Criminal Law Art. 285, para. 2 (illegally controlling a computer information system: up to 3 years, or 3–7 if "especially serious"). I checked the wording against the Beijing PSB's "首都网警" explainer, republished on The Paper (m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_30499370). The paragraph covers anyone who "对该计算机信息系统实施非法控制，情节严重的". The closest precedent is a Baidu operations engineer who put mining scripts on 155 Baidu servers in 2018. The Haidian court convicted him of 非法控制计算机信息系统罪 and sentenced him to 3 years, and the conviction was upheld on appeal (Jiemian, jiemian.com/article/4120906.html). The judicial interpretation's "serious circumstances" threshold (20 or more systems, or ¥10k in losses) cannot be checked, because the paper gives no scale. I left out Art. 286 (sabotage causing abnormal operation), because the paper reports no disruption beyond cost.
